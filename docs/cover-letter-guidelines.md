# Cover Letter Guidelines

Guidelines for writing and reviewing job application cover letters.

## Core Philosophy

**The resume does the heavy lifting.** The cover letter's job is to:
1. Confirm you're applying for this specific job
2. Show you can communicate like a human
3. Express genuine interest
4. Get out of the way

## Length

**Target: 3-5 sentences.** Short and clean beats long and polished.

A cover letter should not repeat resume content. If a story or qualification is already in the resume, don't duplicate it in the cover letter.

## Structure

```
Dear Hiring Manager,

[1 sentence: State the position you're applying for]

[1-2 sentences: Why this is a good fit - keep it simple]

[1 sentence: Thank them, express interest in next steps]

Sincerely,
Joel Lithgow
```

## AI-Detection Red Flags to Avoid

These patterns make recruiters assume your letter was AI-generated:

- **Quoting the company's mission statement back to them** - Everyone does this
- **"Resonates deeply with my commitment to..."** - Classic AI phrasing
- **Overly smooth paragraph structure** - Real humans have edges
- **Hedging language that's too polished** - "I want to be direct about..." sounds rehearsed
- **Perfect keyword alignment** - Reads like it was optimized, not written

## What Works

- **Confidence without over-justification** - "This is a great fit" vs explaining why for 4 paragraphs
- **Clean, direct language** - Say it simply
- **Brevity** - Respect the reader's time
- **Letting the resume speak** - That's where the evidence lives

## Examples

### Too Long / AI-Sounding
> "I am writing to express my interest in the Frontend Developer position. SBAC's mission—that 'a high-quality assessment system can provide information and tools for teachers and schools to improve instruction and help students succeed, regardless of disability, language, or subgroup'—resonates deeply with my commitment to accessible, equitable educational technology. The phrase 'regardless of disability' stands out to me because accessibility has been central to my work..."

### Clean Version
> "I am writing to express my interest in the Frontend Developer position with the Smarter Balanced Assessment Consortium at UC Santa Cruz.
>
> This position is a great fit for my experience and background. I care about accessible educational technology, and SBAC's mission aligns with that.
>
> Thank you for considering my application. I look forward to next steps."

## When to Add More

If you have ONE genuinely memorable detail that's NOT in the resume and that only you could say, you might add it. But default to short.

The bar: Would a recruiter remember this detail after reading 100 applications? If not, cut it.
