## About This Opportunity

Cursor is building an AI-first code editor and looking for a Product Designer to own end-to-end design for features that define how humans and AI collaborate to build software. The role involves tackling complex UX challenges at the intersection of AI capabilities and developer intent, building interaction patterns and design systems, and validating designs with data and user feedback.

They want 3+ years of product design experience, systems thinking, comfort with ambiguity, and understanding of code/AI/developer workflows.

## Why I'm Excited

1. **Designing AI Interaction** - Human-AI collaboration in code editors is genuinely novel UX territory with few established patterns
2. **User Perspective** - I use Cursor daily, so I'd be designing for myself and people like me
3. **End-to-End Ownership** - The role matches how I already work: concept through implementation
4. **Developer Tool UX** - Developer tools reward precision and intentionality in design — my kind of problem
5. **Strong Opinions, Loosely Held** - The posting explicitly values this, and it's how I operate

## Honest Assessment

**Strong match:** End-to-end design ownership, developer tool fluency, data-informed design process, and genuine user perspective on the product.

**Gaps to acknowledge:**
- **Formal product design years** — The posting asks for 3+ years. I have ~2.5 years as a titled UX Designer, though my 7-year trajectory has always involved design work.
- **Consumer/enterprise scale** — My design work has been at a single organization (CCA), not at consumer product scale.
- **Design team experience** — I've been a solo designer/developer, not part of a multi-designer product team with established review processes.

**Why apply anyway:** The posting says "understanding of code, AI, and developer workflows (or willingness to learn)" — I have all three already. Most product designers applying won't be able to prototype in code or have daily experience with the product category. My design-to-code bridge means faster iteration and more feasible designs.
